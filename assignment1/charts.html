Assignment 1: Human Genetic Data

Data Visualization Portfolio · DSC 502

This project explores patterns of genetic variation using principal component analysis (PCA) and interactive Vega-Lite visualizations.

About the Analysis

Principal component analysis (PCA) is a dimensionality reduction technique that transforms many variables into a smaller number of new variables called principal components. The first principal component captures the greatest amount of variation in the data, while the second captures the greatest remaining variation subject to being orthogonal to the first.

In genetic data, PCA can summarize patterns of variation across many genetic markers. Individuals with similar genetic profiles tend to occupy nearby positions in principal-component space. When genetic variation is associated with population history or ancestry, groups of individuals may therefore form distinguishable clusters.

Visualization 1

PC1 vs. PC2

This scatterplot displays the first two principal components for all observations in the dataset. Each point represents an individual, with PC1 and PC2 mapped to the horizontal and vertical axes. The visualization provides a baseline view of the distribution of the observations in principal-component space without introducing any additional categorical encoding.

Visualization 2

PC1 vs. PC2 with Ancestry

This visualization retains the PC1 and PC2 axes from the first plot but adds ancestry as a color encoding. This makes it easier to identify how observations belonging to different ancestry categories are distributed across principal-component space. Compared with the first plot, the additional color encoding makes the relationship between ancestry categories and the observed clustering easier to see.

Visualization 3

PC1 vs. PC2 with Ancestry and |PC3|

This visualization retains the PC1 and PC2 axes and the ancestry color encoding while adding the magnitude of PC3 as point size. PC3 represents a third direction of variation that is not displayed in the first two components, allowing an additional quantitative dimension to be shown simultaneously. Larger circles represent observations with greater absolute PC3 values, while the tooltip provides the original signed PC3 value. The additional encoding provides more information but can also make individual observations somewhat more difficult to distinguish.

Visualization 4

PC1 vs. PC2 by Ancestry

This visualization separates the observations into five ancestry-specific panels while retaining PC1 and PC2 as the horizontal and vertical axes. Unlike the color-based representation in Visualization 2, this design reduces visual overlap between ancestry groups and makes the distribution within each group easier to examine. The panels use identical PC1 and PC2 scales so that the positions and spreads of the groups can be compared directly.
