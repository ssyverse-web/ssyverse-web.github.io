Assignment 1: Human Genetic Data

Data Visualization Portfolio · DSC 502

This project explores patterns of genetic variation using principal component analysis (PCA) and interactive Vega-Lite visualizations.

About the Analysis

Principal component analysis (PCA) is a dimensionality reduction technique that transforms many variables into a smaller number of new variables called principal components. The first principal component captures the greatest amount of variation in the data, while the second captures the greatest remaining variation subject to being independent of the first.

In genetic data, PCA can summarize patterns of variation across many genetic markers. Individuals with similar genetic profiles tend to occupy nearby positions in principal-component space. When genetic variation is associated with population history or ancestry, groups of individuals may therefore form distinguishable clusters.

Visualization 1

PC1 vs. PC2

This scatterplot displays the first two principal components for all observations in the dataset. Each point represents an individual, with PC1 and PC2 mapped to the horizontal and vertical axes. The visualization provides a baseline view of the distribution of the observations in principal-component space without introducing any additional categorical encoding.
